Ask first, pitch second

The outgoing call playbook

A cold or warm call has seven stages. Follow them in order and you'll sound calm, useful and prepared. Scripts are starting points: change the words, keep the structure.

Calling ground rules: Respect Do Not Disturb registrations and permitted calling hours, and stop calling anyone who asks you to. Confirm the current rules with your manager.
1
5 min before

Prepare

  • Open the client's website (or note that they don't have one), Google Maps listing, and Instagram or Facebook page.
  • Write one observation: “Site isn't mobile friendly”, “No contact form”, “Great reviews but no website”.
  • Decide the goal of the call: book a follow-up, collect requirements, or close a package.
  • Keep the Packages tab open and your notes ready.
2
First 20 seconds

Open with permission

State who you are, why you're calling, and ask for a moment. Asking permission lowers resistance immediately.

Say“Hi, is this [Name]? This is [You] from Carrow Marketing. I know I'm calling out of the blue. Do you have two minutes, or is there a better time?”

If they say “I'm busy”: “No problem. When's a better time today or tomorrow? I'll call then.” Note the time and do call back.

3
Next 30 seconds

Give a reason to keep listening

One sentence about them, not us.

Say“I was looking at [business name] online and noticed [your observation]. We help businesses like yours turn that into more enquiries, so I wanted to ask you a couple of quick questions.”
4
3 to 6 minutes

Discover

This is where the sale is won. Ask, then stay quiet. Aim for the client to talk about 70% of the time. The full question bank is in the Discovery & selling tab.

  • “How do new customers find you today?”
  • “What would a great website do for your business in the next six months?”
  • “What's stopped you from getting one done so far?”

Write down their exact words. You'll repeat them in the next step.

5
1 to 2 minutes

Recommend one package

Recommend one, not three. Link it to what they just told you. Mention a second option only if they ask for it or push back on price.

Say“You said [their goal] and that customers mostly find you through [channel]. Based on that, I'd suggest our [Basic / Standard / Premium] package because it gives you [two matching features]. It's ₹[price] and takes [days].”
6
Whenever it comes up

Handle objections with LAAR

Listen. Let them finish. Don't interrupt.

Acknowledge. “That's a fair concern.”

Ask. “Just so I understand, is it the price, or the timing?”

Respond. Answer only the real concern, briefly, then ask if that helps.

Ready-made answers are in the FAQ tab under “Objections”. An objection usually means “I'm interested but not yet sure”, not “no”.

7
Last minute

Close with a specific next step

If they're ready“Great. I'll send you the order link and a short form for your logo, text and photos. Can you have those to us by Friday?”
If they need time“Understood. I'll send you a one-page summary today. Shall we talk again on [day] at [time] to answer any questions?”

After the call

Follow-up rhythm

  • Within 1 hour: WhatsApp or email summary of what you discussed and the agreed next step.
  • Day 2: Answer any question they raised. Add one useful thing (a similar example, a tip).
  • Day 5: Short check-in: “Any questions on the [package]?”
  • Day 10: Final polite follow-up, then note as “nurture”.

Notes to log every time

  • Name, business, best time to call
  • Their goal in their own words
  • Package discussed and objections raised
  • Personality style (see Customer types)
  • Agreed next step and date

Tricky call situations

Gatekeeper (receptionist, assistant)

Say“Hi, I'm [You] from Carrow Marketing. I'd like a quick word with whoever looks after your website or marketing. Who would that be?”

Be polite and honest. Ask for the best time to reach the decision-maker.

Voicemail

Say“Hi [Name], [You] from Carrow Marketing. I had an idea about getting you more enquiries from your website. I'll send a short WhatsApp with details. My number is [number].”

Keep it under 20 seconds. Say your number slowly.

“Send me details”

Agree, then anchor a next step: “Happy to. So I send the right thing, what matters most to you: price, speed, or features? I'll follow up Thursday to see if it makes sense.”

“Not interested”

Don't argue. One gentle question: “Understood. Is that because you already have a website you're happy with, or the timing isn't right?” If it's still no, thank them and close warmly.

Voice habits that help

  • Smile while you speak. It changes your tone.
  • Speak slightly slower than normal.
  • Use the client's name and their words.
  • Pause after a question and let them answer.
  • Reading a script word for word
  • Filling silence when they're thinking
  • Jargon (“CRO”, “SEO”) without explaining it
  • Promising rankings, sales or dates you can't guarantee

Customer types: spot them and speak their language

People buy in four main styles. Find the style in the first minute, then adjust your pace, detail and tone. Treat it as a starting guess, not a label.

Ask one question to reveal their style

Say“How do you usually decide on something like this?”
  • Analytical: “I compare options and check details.”
  • Driver: “I decide fast if it makes sense.”
  • Amiable: “I talk to people I trust first.”
  • Expressive: “I go with what excites me.”

Discovery and how to sell

People don't buy websites. They buy the result the website gives them. Your job on the call is to find that result and connect our package to it.

Five things to find out on every call

Who their customer is

Age, location, how they search, what they worry about.

What problem they solve

Why would someone choose them over the shop next door?

How customers find them now

Word of mouth, Instagram, Google, Justdial, walk-ins. This shows the gap a site fills.

What a new customer is worth

One customer worth ₹20,000 changes how they see a ₹34,680 package.

What has stopped them

Cost, time, past bad experience, not knowing where to start. Name it, then solve it.

Question bank

Use the four groups in order. They move the client from describing their situation to wanting a solution (this is the SPIN method).

Situation: understand the business

  • “Tell me about your business and who your typical customer is.”
  • “Do you have a website now? When was it last updated?”
  • “How do people usually contact you?”

Problem: find the pain

  • “What frustrates you most about how customers find you?”
  • “Do you lose enquiries because people can't find details or a way to reach you?”
  • “Do you compare yourself to competitors who look more professional online?”

Implication: make it matter

  • “If ten people a month can't find you, what's that worth?”
  • “How does that affect your growth plans for this year?”
  • “What happens if a competitor gets that customer instead?”

Need-payoff: let them say the benefit

  • “If your website brought in 10 more enquiries a month, what would that mean for you?”
  • “How would it feel to have bookings coming in without chasing?”
  • “What would make this project a success in six months?”

Never guarantee results. The uplift depends on the business, traffic and follow-up. Ask the client to give their own realistic estimate.

Buying signals and warning signs

Signals to close

  • They ask about price, timing or what happens next
  • They start saying “when we do this…”
  • They ask for examples or how to pay
  • They bring in a partner or decision-maker

Warning signs

  • Vague answers to every question
  • Only asks for the lowest price, with no goal
  • Never available for a second call
  • Wants “everything” for a Basic budget

Stay polite. Offer the package that fits their budget, or agree to reconnect later.

Helping clients sell to their customers

Clients often ask, “But how will people buy from me?” A simple three-part answer:

Audience

Who exactly is the buyer? A specific person is easier to reach than “everyone”.

Offer

What do they get, how much, and why now? A clear offer beats a clever design.

Channel

Where does that buyer already spend time: Google, Instagram, WhatsApp? Put the offer there and send them to the website.

What is marketing?

Marketing is everything a business does to get the right people to notice it, trust it and buy from it, and then come back. It isn't just advertising. It includes what you sell, its price, where it's available, and how you talk about it (the classic “4 Ps”: product, price, place, promotion).

AwarenessPeople discover you: search, social posts, ads, referrals.
ConsiderationThey compare you: website, reviews, portfolio, prices.
ConversionThey act: call, fill a form, book, buy.
RetentionThey return and refer: email, WhatsApp, great service.

Where we come in: the website is the place where awareness turns into enquiries. Every channel (ads, social, Google) usually sends people to it, so a weak site wastes the effort spent elsewhere.

How marketing works with modern algorithms

Google, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and even AI assistants all use ranking or recommendation systems (algorithms). Their job is to show each person what they're most likely to find useful. The details change constantly, but the logic stays steady.

1. They predict interest

Algorithms look at what a person searched, watched, saved and clicked, then rank content they'll probably want.

2. They reward useful content

Content people finish, save, share or click and don't immediately leave tends to travel further. Clear, helpful pages beat keyword stuffing.

3. They watch behaviour

If visitors bounce from a slow or confusing page, that's a signal the page isn't helping. Experience is part of visibility.

Five shifts every seller should understand

Attention is earned in seconds

Feeds move fast. If the first moments don't grab people, the algorithm stops showing the content.

Helpful beats clever

Search and AI systems favour content written for people: specific, honest and easy to understand.

Speed and mobile are table stakes

Most browsing is on phones. Slow pages lose visitors and can weaken search performance.

Your own audience is an asset

Platform rules and reach change. Email and WhatsApp lists and your own website don't disappear when an algorithm shifts.

Trust decides conversion

Reviews, a real portfolio, clear contact details and professional design all tell visitors “safe to buy”.

Nobody can promise rankings

Nobody outside the platform controls the algorithm. Never guarantee “Page 1” or a specific number of leads.
